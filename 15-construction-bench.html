    Notes on this bench

    How the tools behave

    Every tool is two clicks. The ruler takes a start and an end. The compasses take an opening from the tray, then a centre. The protractor takes the vertex, picks up the line you are measuring from, then the angle. The set square takes a line, then the point the new line must pass through.

    What counts as correct

    The sheet measures the lines, not the drawing: 1.5° on an angle and 2 mm on a length. The compasses task also asks for the arcs, because the arcs are the evidence that the vertex was found, not guessed.

    The five cases

    Three sides: ruler and compasses. Two sides and the angle between them: protractor. Two angles and the side between them: protractor. Two angles and a side that is not between them: find the third angle first. A right angle, the hypotenuse and a side: set square and compasses.

    Three questions to ask the class
    1. On task 3, why does the vertex have to be exactly where the two arcs cross, and not merely near it?
    2. On task 6, the angle at P is not given. What tells you it must be 45°?
    3. On task 7, you are given a side, a right angle and the hypotenuse. Why can the hypotenuse never be the shortest side?
    Where this sits in the chapter

    This bench practises plate 5.3, Basic Constructions. The method for each task is written out on that plate; the bench is where you find out whether you can actually do it with the instruments in your hand.
