Parallel & Perpendicular Lines

平行線與垂直線 — 符號與標記

Learning objectives

  1. Understand what parallel lines and perpendicular lines are.
  2. Write them with the symbols // and ⊥.
  3. Recognise the arrowhead and right-angle markings in a figure.
  4. Use the markings to name pairs of parallel / perpendicular lines.

1Parallel lines 平行線

Two lines in the same plane that never meet, however far you extend them, are parallel. They are always the same distance apart.

Definition

Parallel lines are lines in the same plane that do not intersect, no matter how far they are extended. Their distance apart is constant.

Symbol: AB // CD  read as “AB is parallel to CD”.

Marking: matching arrowheads on the lines. One arrow on each line = one pair; two arrows on each line = another pair.

Real life: railway tracks, the lines of a ruled page, the two rails of a ladder, opposite edges of a door.

Why the arrowheads matter

In a figure with several lines, the arrowheads are the only reliable evidence of parallel lines. Lines with one arrow are parallel to each other; lines with two arrows are parallel to each other; a one-arrow line is not necessarily parallel to a two-arrow line.

2Perpendicular lines 垂直線

Two lines that meet at a right angle (90°) are perpendicular.

Definition

Perpendicular lines are two lines that intersect at a right angle (90°).

Symbol: AB ⊥ CD

Marking: a small square at the corner where they meet. If you see the square, the angle is 90° — you do not need a number.

Real life: the corner of a book, a wall meeting the floor, the upright of a door frame, the cross of a window.

Do not confuse the two symbols

// = never meet  ·  ⊥ = meet at exactly 90°. They are complete opposites — one means “no meeting point”, the other means “meets at a perfect corner”.

3Notation summary 符號總結

SymbolMeaningRead asMarked in a figure by

AB = CDthe two segments have equal length“AB equals CD”matching tick marks (—) on both segments
Reading a figure in three steps
  1. Look for arrows → those lines are parallel.
  2. Look for squares → those lines are perpendicular.
  3. Look for tick marks → those segments are equal in length.

4Worked examples 例題

Example 1Two groups of parallel lines

In the figure there are two groups of parallel lines. AB and EF form one group; CD and GH form the other.

(a) Write down a pair of parallel lines.
(b) Which two lines are not parallel to AB?

(a) AB // EF  (one arrowhead on each). Another correct answer: CD // GH  (two arrowheads on each).

(b) CD and GH — they carry two arrowheads, so they belong to the other group.

Example 2Perpendicular lines in a square

The figure shows a square ABCD.

(a) Write down a pair of perpendicular lines in square ABCD.
(b) Write down a pair of parallel lines in square ABCD.

(a) AB ⊥ BC — they meet at the right angle at B. (Any adjacent pair works: BC ⊥ CD, CD ⊥ DA, DA ⊥ AB.)

(b) AB // DC and AD // BC.

5Let's check 課堂練習

Check yourself

Look at the rectangle below. All four corners are right angles.

  1. Write down two pairs of parallel lines.
  2. Write down all pairs of perpendicular lines that meet at P.
  3. Is PQ // SR true? Is PQ // PS true?
  1. PQ // SR and PS // QR.
  2. PQ ⊥ PS (the right angle is at P).
  3. PQ // SR is true; PQ // PS is false — they meet at P, so they are perpendicular, not parallel.

!Common mistakes 常見錯誤

✗ WrongUsing // for lines that meet
✓ RightMeeting at 90° → ⊥
If the lines cross, they are not parallel. If they cross at a right angle, write ⊥.
✗ WrongMarking only one line with an arrowhead
✓ RightMark both lines with the same arrowhead
The pair of matching marks is what declares the two lines parallel. One mark alone means nothing.
✗ Wrong“They look parallel, so AB // CD”
✓ RightOnly if the figure marks them, or the question says so
Figures are not drawn to scale. Unmarked lines may be slightly tilted on purpose.
✗ WrongAB // CD // EF when AB has one arrow and EF has two
✓ RightOnly lines with the same number of arrowheads are parallel
Different arrow counts = different directions. This is a favourite trap in multiple-choice questions.
✗ WrongWriting AB ⊥ CD = 90°
✓ RightAB ⊥ CD (and, if needed, “the angle between them is 90°”)
⊥ is a relationship between two lines, not a size. Never put “= 90°” after it.
One-line summary

Parallel = same direction, never meet, marked with matching arrows. Perpendicular = meet at 90°, marked with a small square.
