3-D Figures

立體圖形 — 多面體、稜柱、稜錐、球體、截面

Learning objectives

  1. Know the two types of solids: polyhedra and non-polyhedra.
  2. Understand prisms, pyramids, circular cylinders, circular cones and spheres.
  3. Understand cross-sections and uniform cross-sections.
  4. Sketch the 2-D representation of a solid, with hidden edges dotted.

1From flat to solid 由平面到立體

A solid is a 3-D object. It has three things to count: faces, edges and vertices.

Face 面

A flat surface of the solid. A cube has 6 square faces. A face is 2-D — it has area but no thickness.

Edge 稜

A line segment where two faces meet. A cube has 12 edges.

Vertex 頂點

A point where edges meet — a corner. A cube has 8 vertices.

Why count them?

Counting faces, edges and vertices is the fastest way to describe a solid precisely. It also tells you immediately whether a solid is a polyhedron.

2Polyhedra and non-polyhedra 多面體與非多面體

Definition — learn it word for word

A polyhedron (多面體, plural polyhedra) is a solid enclosed by polygons only. Every face is a flat polygon — there is no curved surface anywhere.

✓ Polyhedra — all faces are polygons

✗ Non-polyhedra — they have a curved surface

The classic question: “Is a sphere a polyhedron?”

No. A sphere has no flat faces at all — its whole surface is curved. The same is true for a cylinder, a cone and a hemisphere. If a solid has even one curved surface, it is not a polyhedron.

3Prisms 稜柱

A prism has two identical, parallel faces (the bases) and flat rectangular side faces joining them. Its cross-section is the same all the way through.

How to describe a prism

  • Bases — two identical polygons, parallel to each other.
  • Lateral faces — rectangles joining the two bases.
  • Named by its base: triangular prism, rectangular prism, pentagonal prism.
Naming rule

Look at the base, not the long side. A “triangular prism” is one whose base are triangles — the three long faces are rectangles.

Everyday prisms

A Toblerone box (triangular prism), a brick (rectangular prism), a hexagonal pencil before it is sharpened (hexagonal prism), a fish tank (rectangular prism).

4Pyramids 稜錐

How to describe a pyramid

  • Base — one polygon (any number of sides).
  • Apex (錐頂) — a single point at the top, above the base.
  • Lateral faces — all triangles, meeting at the apex.
  • Named by its base: triangular pyramid, square pyramid, pentagonal pyramid.

A triangular pyramid has only 4 faces, so it is also called a tetrahedron (四面體).

A cone is NOT a pyramid

A cone has a curved lateral surface, so it is not a polyhedron and not a pyramid. A pyramid’s lateral faces are flat triangles. They look similar, but one has curved faces and the other does not.

5Cylinder, cone and sphere 圓柱、圓錐、球

Circular cylinder 圓柱

2 circular ends + 1 curved surface.
Uniform cross-sections (circles).

Circular cone 圓錐

1 circular base, 1 apex, 1 curved surface.
Cross-sections shrink towards the apex.

Sphere 球

1 completely curved surface.
No vertices, no edges, no flat faces.

7Cross-sections 截面

When you cut a solid with a flat plane, the exposed surface is called a cross-section. Think of slicing a loaf of bread: every slice is a cross-section.

Two definitions

Cross-section (截面): the plane figure you get when a solid is cut by a plane.

Uniform cross-section (均勻橫截面): a solid has uniform cross-sections if the cross-sections all have the same shape and size as its base, when the solid is cut by any plane parallel to the base.

The sphere trap

Every cross-section of a sphere is a circle — but the circles are different sizes. So a sphere has circular cross-sections, yet it does not have uniform cross-sections. “Uniform” needs the same shape and the same size.

The quick test

Prism or cylinder → yes. Pyramid, cone or sphere → no. The shape has to stay exactly the same size all the way through; anything that tapers (to an apex or to a point on the surface) fails.

8Drawing a solid in 2-D 立體圖形的平面表示

On paper a solid is only a picture. Two conventions make the picture readable.

Two rules

  1. Hidden edges are drawn dotted (or dashed). They are the edges you could not see if the solid were real.
  2. Parallel edges stay parallel on paper, and equal edges are drawn equal in length.

Why the dotted lines matter

A cube drawn with only the visible edges looks like three squares glued together. The dotted edges are what make it a solid. In a test, forgetting the dotted lines usually costs a mark.

9Worked examples 例題

Example 1Is it a polyhedron?

Decide whether each solid is a polyhedron: (a) a cube, (b) a sphere, (c) a triangular prism, (d) a circular cone.

(a) Yes — six square faces, all polygons.

(b) No — the whole surface is curved.

(c) Yes — two triangles and three rectangles.

(d) No — the lateral surface is curved.

Example 2Count vertices, edges and faces

Write down the number of vertices, edges and faces of (a) a triangular prism, (b) a square pyramid.

(a) Triangular prism: 6 vertices, 9 edges, 5 faces (2 triangles + 3 rectangles).

(b) Square pyramid: 5 vertices (4 base corners + the apex), 8 edges (4 base + 4 slanting), 5 faces (1 square + 4 triangles).

A reliable way to count edges of a prism: 3 × (number of sides of the base). For a triangular prism: 3 × 3 = 9. For a pentagonal prism: 3 × 5 = 15.

Example 3Cross-section reasoning

A solid has a square base. When it is cut by a plane parallel to that base, every cross-section is the same square.

(a) What kind of solid could it be?   (b) Could it be a square pyramid?

(a) A prism with a square base — a cuboid (or a cube). It has uniform cross-sections.

(b) No. In a square pyramid the cross-sections parallel to the base get smaller as you move towards the apex, so they are not the same size.

!Common mistakes 常見錯誤

✗ Wrong“A sphere is a polyhedron because it is a solid.”
✓ RightNo — a polyhedron must be enclosed by polygons only
A sphere has no flat face at all. Curved surface = not a polyhedron.
✗ Wrong“A cone is a pyramid with a circular base.”
✓ RightA cone is not a pyramid
A pyramid’s lateral faces must be flat triangles. A cone’s surface is curved.
✗ Wrong“A pyramid has uniform cross-sections because all its slices are squares.”
✓ RightThe slices must be the same size, not just the same shape
“Uniform” means same shape and same size. A pyramid’s slices shrink.
✗ WrongNaming a prism by its long rectangular face
✓ RightName it by its base
A triangular prism has 3 rectangular lateral faces and 2 triangular ends.
✗ WrongDrawing all edges as solid lines
✓ RightHidden edges are dotted
Without the dotted edges the picture looks flat and the sketch loses a mark.
✗ Wrong“A cube has 12 faces.”
✓ RightA cube has 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices
Faces = flat surfaces; edges = lines where faces meet. Mixing them up is very common.
One-line summary

Polyhedron = flat polygon faces only. Prism = two identical parallel ends + rectangular sides (uniform cross-section). Pyramid = one base + apex (no uniform cross-section). Sphere = all curved, every slice a circle but the circles change size (not uniform).
