Learning objectives
  1. Understand the concept of a triangle and its parts.
  2. Classify triangles by the lengths of the sides.
  3. Classify triangles by the sizes of the angles.
  4. Recognise that the sum of the interior angles of a triangle is 180° and use it to find unknowns.

1What is a triangle? 甚麼是三角形

A triangle is the simplest polygon: three straight sides, three vertices and three interior angles.

Parts of a triangle

  • Vertices (頂點): A, B, C: always capital letters.
  • Sides (邊): AB, BC, CA.
  • Interior angles (內角): ∠A, ∠B, ∠C: inside the triangle.

Notation: the triangle with vertices A, B, C is written △ABC and read “triangle ABC”.

Watch the symbol

△ is a triangle; ∠ is an angle. △ABC is the whole shape, ∠ABC is just one corner of it.

Naming rule

Name a triangle by its three vertices: △ABC, △BCA and △CAB are the same triangle. △ACB is also the same: any order works, as long as all three letters appear. (This is different from angles, where the vertex must be in the middle.)

2Classified by the lengths of the sides 按邊長分類

Equilateral triangle 等邊三角形

All three sides are equal.
Each interior angle is 60°.

Isosceles triangle 等腰三角形

Two sides are equal.
The two angles at the base are equal.

Scalene triangle 不等邊三角形

All three sides are unequal.
All three angles are different.

The tick marks tell you

In a figure, equal sides are marked with matching tick marks (the little dashes). One dash on two sides = those two sides are equal. Two dashes = a different pair of equal sides. Never judge equality by looking.

3Classified by the sizes of the angles 按角的大小分類

Acute-angled triangle 銳角三角形

All three angles are acute (less than 90°).

Right-angled triangle 直角三角形

One angle is a right angle. The side opposite it is the hypotenuse (斜邊), the longest side.

Obtuse-angled triangle 鈍角三角形

One angle is an obtuse angle (between 90° and 180°).

A triangle can have two names

The two ways of classifying are independent. A triangle with a right angle and two equal sides is an isosceles right-angled triangle (等腰直角三角形). The one in the figure below has a right angle at B, with AB = BC.

4Angle sum of a triangle 三角形內角和

Key point: the most used fact in the chapter

The sum of all the interior angles of a triangle is 180°.

In △ABC,   ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°

Abbreviation to write: (∠ sum of △)

Try it yourself

Move the two sliders. The third angle is always forced to make the total 180°.

55°
65°
60°

Extension · why 180° and not 360°?

A square has 4 right angles: 4 × 90° = 360°. Cut the square along a diagonal and you get two triangles, each taking half of the 360°: 360° ÷ 2 = 180°. This is the idea behind the polygon formula you will meet later.

5Worked examples 例題

Example 1One unknown angle

In △ABC, ∠A = 42° and ∠B = 73°. Find ∠C.

In △ABC,

∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°   (∠ sum of △)

42° + 73° + ∠C = 180°

∠C = 180° − 115° = 65°

∠C = 65°

All three angles are acute, so △ABC is an acute-angled triangle.

Example 2Isosceles triangle with x

In △PQR, PQ = PR and ∠P = 112°. Find ∠Q.

Since PQ = PR, the two base angles are equal: ∠Q = ∠R = x.

In △PQR,

112° + x + x = 180°   (∠ sum of △)

2x = 68°

x = 34°, so ∠Q = 34°

Example 3Two steps: angle sum, then straight line

In the figure, QRS is a straight line, ∠QPR = 52°, ∠PQR = 64° and ∠PSR = 30°. Find x and y.

Hint from the textbook: work on the triangle that has only one unknown angle first.

Step 1: find x. In △PQR,

∠PRQ = 180° − 52° − 64° = 64°   (∠ sum of △)

Since QRS is a straight line, x and this 64° are adjacent angles on it:

x + 64° = 180°   (adj. ∠s on st. line)

x = 116°

Step 2: find y. In △PRS,

y + 116° + 30° = 180°   (∠ sum of △)

y = 34°

x = 116°, y = 34°

6Let’s check 課堂練習

Check 1 · can these be the angles of a triangle?

Add the three angles. If the total is exactly 180°, and none of them is 0°: they can.

  1. 40°, 50°, 90°
  2. 100°, 30°, 60°
  3. 80°, 100°, 0°
  4. 8°, 163°, 9°
  1. Yes: 40 + 50 + 90 = 180. (It is a right-angled triangle.)
  2. No: 100 + 30 + 60 = 190.
  3. No: the total is 180, but an angle of 0° is impossible; the triangle would be flat.
  4. Yes: 8 + 163 + 9 = 180. (Obtuse-angled, since 163° > 90°.)
Check 2 · classify and calculate
  1. A triangle has angles 90°, 45°, 45°. Give its full name.
  2. A triangle has sides 5 cm, 5 cm, 8 cm. What type is it by its sides? What do you know about two of its angles?
  3. In △ABC, ∠A = ∠B and ∠C = 100°. Find ∠A.
  4. Can a triangle have two right angles? Explain.
  1. An isosceles right-angled triangle (two equal angles → two equal sides).
  2. Isosceles. The two angles opposite the equal sides are equal.
  3. ∠A + ∠B = 180° − 100° = 80°, and they are equal, so ∠A = 40°.
  4. No: two right angles already total 180°, leaving 0° for the third angle, which is impossible.

!Common mistakes 常見錯誤

✗ Wrong“An isosceles triangle is an equilateral triangle.”
✓ RightAn equilateral triangle is a isosceles triangle
Isosceles needs only two equal sides. Equilateral needs three. The arrow of truth points one way only.
✗ Wrong“A right-angled triangle cannot be isosceles.”
✓ Right90°, 45°, 45° is a perfectly good isosceles right-angled triangle
The two classifications are independent: a triangle can carry one name from each list.
✗ WrongUsing 360° for the angle sum
✓ Right180° for a triangle
360° is for angles at a point (or a quadrilateral). A triangle is always 180°.
✗ Wrong“It looks equilateral, so all angles are 60°.”
✓ RightOnly if the sides are marked equal
Check for tick marks. Unmarked sides are not equal just because they look it.
✗ WrongForgetting the reason in brackets
✓ RightWrite (∠ sum of △) or (adj. ∠s on st. line)
In a long question the reason earns a method mark. It also proves you know why the step is true.
One-line summary

Three sides, three angles, one fact: they add to 180°. Classify by sides (equilateral / isosceles / scalene) and by angles (acute / right / obtuse), and a triangle may have one name from each list.
