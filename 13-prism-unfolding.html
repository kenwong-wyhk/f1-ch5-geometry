Unfolding a Prism 稜柱的展開圖 — 側面、底面與表面積

A prism is a flat shape given a thickness. Pull the hinges open and its surface flattens into a net: one long rectangle for the sides, plus the two bases as lids. Change the base and the height, and watch which number on the right does the moving.

Live model · drag to orbit · scroll to zoom · slide “Unfold” to fold it back up Open full screen ↗

What you are looking at

The lateral surface is not one surface — it is n separate rectangles, hinged edge to edge. Open every hinge flat and they line up into a single long rectangle. The two bases swing out on their own hinges, like lids.

Try this

Set the base to 3 sides, then to 8. The strip gets longer every time but never taller: its width is the whole perimeter of the base.

Now move Prism height. Only the height of the strip changes — the bases do not move at all.

The rule it is showing

lateral area = perimeter × height

surface area = lateral + 2 × base area

The panel also computes volume as base area × height — that formula belongs to a later chapter, so treat it here as a preview.

Two questions to ask the class
  1. A triangular prism and a hexagonal prism are both 10 cm tall. Which one needs the longer strip of paper to wrap its sides, and how many times longer?
  2. Fold the strip back up. Why does every rectangle in the strip keep the same height, no matter how many sides the base has?
Beyond Chapter 5 · 延伸

Surface area and volume of prisms are not assessed in Chapter 5 — the chapter only asks you to describe solids and their cross-sections. This lab is here so that the formulas arrive later with a picture already attached to them.
