1What is an angle? 甚麼是角

An angle measures how much you turn. When two line segments start from the same point, they form an angle.

The three parts of an angle

  • Vertex (頂點): the common endpoint, here B.
  • Arms (邊): the two line segments, here BA and BC.
  • Size: measured in degrees (°), written ∠ABC = 40°.

The size of an angle does not depend on how long the arms are. Lengthen the arms and the angle stays the same.

Key point

An angle is formed by two line segments with a common endpoint called the vertex. The size of the angle is the amount of turning from one arm to the other.

2Naming an angle: the vertex goes in the middle 角的命名

This is the single most examined piece of notation in the chapter. Get the order right and you never lose these marks.

Three ways to name the same angle

Take the angle at B between BA and BC:

∠ABCvertex B in the middle ✓
∠CBAsame angle, read the other way ✓
∠Bonly if there is one angle at B
a, b, x, θ, …when the figure labels it with a small letter
The rule

∠ABC and ∠CBA are the same angle.
∠BAC is a different angle (vertex A).
Swap the two outer letters: fine.
Move the vertex: different angle.

Notation check

Write ∠ABC = 40°, not ABC = 40° and not ∠ABC = 40.
The degree sign is part of the answer.

3The six types of angles 角的分類

Every angle you meet in this chapter belongs to one of these six families. Learn the size range, not just the name.

Acute angle 銳角

Greater than 0° and less than 90°

Right angle 直角

Exactly 90°: marked with a small square

Obtuse angle 鈍角

Greater than 90° and less than 180°

Straight angle 平角

Exactly 180°: the two arms form a straight line

Reflex angle 反角

Greater than 180° and less than 360°

Round angle 周角

Exactly 360°: one complete turn

5Measuring an angle 量度角

Using a protractor 量角器

  1. Put the centre mark of the protractor exactly on the vertex.
  2. Line up the 0° line with one arm of the angle.
  3. Read the number where the other arm crosses the scale.
  4. Check: is it acute or obtuse? If your reading says 140° but the angle is clearly acute, you used the wrong scale: read the other set of numbers.

The two scales

A protractor has an inner scale and an outer scale running in opposite directions. Both are correct: you just have to pick the one that starts at 0 on the arm you lined up.

Quick sanity check

Estimate first: is the angle smaller than a right angle (acute) or bigger (obtuse)? If your reading disagrees with your estimate, re-read using the other scale.

(a) a straight angle    (b) a round angle    (c) 2.5 right angles

(a) A straight angle = 180°

(b) A round angle = 360°

(c) 2.5 × 90° = 225°: this is a reflex angle.

Example 2Arrange in ascending order

Arrange in ascending order of size using “<”:   1.5 right angles,   0.6 straight angle,   0.7 round angle.

  1. 1.5 × 90° = 135°
  2. 0.6 × 180° = 108°
  3. 0.7 × 360° = 252°

0.6 straight angle < 1.5 right angles < 0.7 round angle

7Complementary & supplementary angles 餘角與補角

Two angles can be “partners”. The partner of an angle is found by subtraction: 90° for one pair, 180° for the other.

Complementary angles 餘角

Sum = 90° (a right angle)

Supplementary angles 補角

Sum = 180° (a straight angle)

Definitions: learn these two sentences exactly
  1. If the sum of two angles is 90°, the two angles are called complementary angles (餘角).
  2. If the sum of two angles is 180°, the two angles are called supplementary angles (補角).

They do not have to be next to each other: only the sum matters. When they are next to each other they are also called adjacent (鄰角).

Angles on a straight line

When several angles sit side by side on a straight line, they add up to a straight angle:

∠AOB + ∠BOC + ∠COD = 180°

Reason to write: adjacent angles on a straight line (同一直線上的鄰角).

Angles at a point

When angles go all the way round a point, they add up to a round angle:

∠AOB + ∠BOC + ∠COA = 360°

Reason to write: angles at a point (同頂角).

8Worked examples 例題

Example 3Using a supplementary pair to find x

∠AOB and ∠BOC are supplementary angles, where ∠AOB = (2x + 10)° and ∠BOC = (3x − 10)°. Find x and ∠AOB.

  1. Supplementary means the sum is 180°:
    (2x + 10) + (3x − 10) = 180
  2. Simplify: 5x = 180
  3. So x = 36.
  4. Then ∠AOB = 2(36) + 10 = 82°.
  5. Check: ∠BOC = 3(36) − 10 = 98°, and 82° + 98° = 180° ✓

x = 36, ∠AOB = 82°

Example 4Naming pairs from a figure

In the figure, AOD is a straight line. ∠AOB = 30°, ∠BOC = 60° and ∠COD = 90°.

(a) Name a pair of complementary angles.
(b) Name a pair of supplementary angles.

(a) ∠AOB + ∠BOC = 30° + 60° = 90°, so ∠AOB and ∠BOC are complementary angles.

(b) ∠AOB + ∠BOD = 30° + (60° + 90°) = 180°, so ∠AOB and ∠BOD are supplementary angles.
(Also correct: ∠AOC and ∠COD, since 90° + 90° = 180°.)

Add before you name

Never name a pair of complementary / supplementary angles from the picture alone. Add the numbers up first. If the total is not exactly 90° (or 180°), it is not a complementary (or supplementary) pair. Diagrams in tests are not drawn to scale.

9Let’s check 課堂練習

Check 1 · same angle or not?

In the figure, CAD is a straight line and B is a point above it. Decide whether each pair names the same angle.

  1. ∠BCA and ∠BAC
  2. ∠DBA and ∠ABD
  3. ∠BCD and ∠ACB
  4. ∠BDC and ∠BDA
  1. Different. ∠BCA has vertex C; ∠BAC has vertex A.
  2. Same. ∠DBA and ∠ABD both have vertex B, with arms BD and BA: only the outer letters are swapped.
  3. Same. Both have vertex C, with arms CB and the line segment CAD.
  4. Same. Both have vertex D, with arms DB and the line segment DAC.
Check 2 · name the angles

In the figure, DBC is a straight line and ∠a = 40°. Use the symbol ∠ and letters to name each marked angle in two ways (the first is done for you).

a = ∠BAC or ∠CAB

  • b = ∠ABC or ∠CBA  (the angle at B inside the triangle)
  • c = ∠BCA or ∠ACB  (the angle at C inside the triangle)
  • x = ∠ACD or ∠DCA  (the angle outside the triangle, at C)

Why not ∠C? Because there are two angles at C: c and x, so ∠C would be ambiguous.

Check 3 · calculate
  1. Find the complement of 28°.
  2. Find the supplement of 28°.
  3. Express 3.5 right angles in degrees, and name the type of angle.
  1. 90° − 28° = 62°
  2. 180° − 28° = 152°
  3. 3.5 × 90° = 315°: a reflex angle.

!Common mistakes 常見錯誤

✗ Wrong∠BAC for the angle at B
✓ Right∠ABC
The middle letter is always the vertex. ∠BAC is the angle at A.
✗ Wrong∠B when two angles meet at B
✓ Right∠ABD or ∠DBC
One letter is only allowed when there is exactly one angle at that vertex.
✗ WrongComplement of 70° is 110°
✓ RightComplement of 70° is 20°; its supplement is 110°
Complement → 90°. Supplement → 180°. Mixing them is the most common error in this section.
✗ WrongA reflex angle is “a very big obtuse angle”
✓ RightReflex: 180° < angle < 360°; obtuse: 90° < angle < 180°
Obtuse stops at 180°. Anything beyond that is reflex.
✗ Wrong∠ABC = 40
✓ Right∠ABC = 40°
Angles are measured in degrees: the ° is not optional.
✗ WrongAssuming two angles are complementary because they “look” right
✓ RightAdd the numbers: must be exactly 90°
Diagrams in exams are not drawn to scale. Only the given numbers count.
One-line summary

An angle is two line segments from one vertex; name it with the vertex in the middle; measure it in degrees; two angles are complementary at 90° and supplementary at 180°.
