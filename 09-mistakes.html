Common Mistakes

常見錯誤 — 考試前十分鐘看這一頁

How to use this page

  1. Read each wrong → right pair and cover the right-hand side.
  2. Say the reason out loud. If you cannot explain why, go back to that section.
  3. Then do the self-check quiz and see which traps you still fall into.

1Naming & notation 命名與符號

✗ WrongABC = 40°
✓ Right∠ABC = 40°
Without the angle sign, ABC looks like a triangle or a product of three numbers.
✗ Wrong∠BAC for the angle at B
✓ Right∠ABC
The middle letter is the vertex. Always.
✗ Wrong∠B when two angles meet at B
✓ Right∠ABD or ∠DBC
One letter is allowed only when there is exactly one angle at that vertex.
✗ WrongPoint a, point b
✓ RightPoint A, point B
Points use capital letters. Small letters are for lines and angles.
✗ WrongAB = 4 cm where AB is a line
✓ RightAB = 4 cm (a segment has a length)
A line has no endpoints, so it has no length.
✗ WrongAB ⊥ CD = 90°
✓ RightAB ⊥ CD
⊥ describes a relationship, not a size. Never attach “= 90°”.
✗ WrongAB // CD // EF when the arrowheads differ
✓ RightOnly lines with the same arrowhead count are parallel
Different arrow counts mean different directions. A favourite multiple-choice trap.

2Angles 角

✗ WrongComplement of 70° is 110°
✓ RightComplement = 20°; supplement = 110°
Complement → 90°. Supplement → 180°.
✗ Wrong∠ABC = 40
✓ Right∠ABC = 40°
The degree sign is part of the answer.
✗ WrongCalling a 200° angle “obtuse”
✓ Right200° is a reflex angle
Obtuse stops at 180°. Anything from 180° to 360° is reflex.
✗ WrongNaming a complementary pair because the diagram looks right
✓ RightAdd the numbers: they must total exactly 90°
Diagrams are not drawn to scale. Only the given values count.
✗ WrongUsing the wrong protractor scale (reading 140° for an acute angle)
✓ RightEstimate first, then read the scale that starts at 0 on your arm
A protractor has two scales running in opposite directions.

3Triangles 三角形

✗ Wrong“An isosceles triangle is an equilateral triangle.”
✓ RightAn equilateral triangle is a special isosceles triangle
Isosceles needs 2 equal sides; equilateral needs 3. The implication runs one way only.
✗ Wrong“A right-angled triangle cannot be isosceles.”
✓ Right90°, 45°, 45° is an isosceles right-angled triangle
Classification by sides and by angles are independent.
✗ WrongUsing 360° for the angle sum
✓ Right180°
360° belongs to angles at a point. A triangle is always 180°.
✗ WrongAssuming a triangle is equilateral because it looks balanced
✓ RightLook for the tick marks
Equal sides are marked, not implied by appearance.
✗ WrongWriting the answer with no reason in brackets
✓ Right(∠ sum of △), (adj. ∠s on st. line)
The reason earns a method mark and proves you understand the step.

4Polygons 多邊形

✗ Wrong“All sides equal → regular polygon”
✓ RightRegular = equal sides and equal angles
A rhombus is the standard counter-example.
✗ Wrong“A rectangle is a regular polygon”
✓ RightIt is equiangular, not equilateral
Only a square is both.
✗ WrongCounting sides as diagonals
✓ RightDiagonals join non-adjacent vertices
A pentagon has 5 sides and 5 diagonals — do not merge them.
✗ Wrong“Concave means it has a hole”
✓ RightConcave = at least one interior angle > 180°
The test is the angle, not the general look.

5Circles 圓

✗ Wrong“Any segment joining two points on a circle is a diameter.”
✓ RightIt is a chord; a diameter must pass through the centre
A standard multiple-choice trap — the missing condition is hidden inside the wording.
✗ WrongMixing up sector and segment
✓ RightSector = 2 radii + arc; Segment = 1 chord + arc
A sector reaches the centre (pizza slice); a segment does not.
✗ Wrongradius = 2 × diameter
✓ Rightdiameter = 2 × radius
The diameter is the bigger one — it goes all the way across.
✗ Wrong“The arc AB is the straight line from A to B.”
✓ RightThe arc is curved; the straight segment is a chord
Two different objects between the same two points.

63-D figures 立體圖形

✗ Wrong“A sphere is a polyhedron.”
✓ RightNo — a polyhedron is enclosed by polygons only
A sphere has no flat face at all.
✗ Wrong“A cone is a pyramid with a circular base.”
✓ RightA cone is not a pyramid
A pyramid’s lateral faces are flat triangles; a cone’s surface is curved.
✗ Wrong“A pyramid has uniform cross-sections.”
✓ RightNo — its slices get smaller towards the apex
“Uniform” means same shape and same size.
✗ Wrong“A cube has 12 faces.”
✓ Right6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices
Faces = surfaces, edges = lines where faces meet, vertices = corners.
✗ WrongDrawing every edge as a solid line
✓ RightHidden edges are dotted
Without dotted edges the sketch looks flat.

7Constructions 作圖

✗ WrongRubbing out the construction arcs
✓ RightLeave every arc visible
The arcs are the evidence of your method — and they carry marks.
✗ WrongChanging the compass opening between arcs
✓ RightSet each length once and keep it
Otherwise the constructed triangle does not match the given data.
