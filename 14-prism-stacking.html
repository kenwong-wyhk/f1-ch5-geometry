Stacking a Prism 由橫截面堆疊成柱體 — 均勻橫截面與體積

One flat shape, copied and stacked straight up: that is all a prism is. Every slice is the same shape and the same size — the uniform cross-section — and the stack is what makes V = A × h work.

Live model · choose a cross-section, then drag the solid to turn it Open full screen ↗

What you are looking at

Identical copies of one flat shape, stacked straight up. Nothing tapers and nothing twists: every slice is congruent to the base. That is exactly what uniform cross-section means — same shape and same size.

Try this

Switch the cross-section to trapezium, then L-shape. Both are still prisms: the test is that the slice never changes, not that the base is a “nice” shape.

Then press Lay it down — the solid is unchanged, but a side face is now on the floor.

The rule it is showing

volume = area of cross-section × height

V = A × h

The Slices slider changes how many slices you draw, not the volume — thinner slices, same total.

The height is not always the “up” side

Press Lay it down and the prism rests on a rectangular face. The cross-section is still the triangle (or trapezium, or L-shape), but the height is now the prism’s length. The height is always measured perpendicular to the cross-section — that is the only thing that matters.

Two questions to ask the class
  1. Set Slices to 1, then to 40. The picture changes completely — does the volume change? Why not?
  2. A pyramid is stacked from slices too. Why does V = A × h not work for it?
Beyond Chapter 5 · 延伸
