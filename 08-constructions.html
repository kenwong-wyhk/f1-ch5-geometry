Learning objectives
  1. Recognise the tools used in geometric construction.
  2. Construct parallel and perpendicular lines with a ruler and a set square.
  3. Construct a circle and copy a line segment with a pair of compasses.
  4. Construct a triangle from given information (five cases).

1The four tools 四種工具

A construction is a drawing made with instruments only: no guessing, no freehand. Each tool does one job well.

Ruler 直尺

Draws straight lines and measures lengths. Must have a clear, undamaged edge.

Set square 三角尺

A triangle with a right angle (45°, 45° and 90°, or 30°, 60° and 90°). Used with a ruler to draw parallel and perpendicular lines.

Protractor 量角器

Measures and draws angles in degrees. Needed when a triangle is given by its angles.

Compasses 圓規

Draws circles and arcs, and copies a length exactly. The two points must be tight, not wobbly.

Before you start

Sharpen your pencil (a blunt pencil makes a 0.5 mm line into a 2 mm smear, and your construction will not be accurate). Check that the compasses hinge is tight and that the needle point is sharp.

2Parallel and perpendicular lines 作平行線與垂直線

Key point

Parallel lines and perpendicular lines can always be constructed using a ruler and a set square.

Constructing a perpendicular through a point

Constructing a parallel through a point

  1. Place one edge of the set square along the given line AB.
  2. Place the ruler against the other edge of the set square (the hypotenuse) and hold the ruler still.
  3. Slide the set square along the ruler until its first edge passes through the point P.
  4. Draw the line along that edge. It is parallel to AB.
Why it works

Sliding a rigid set square along a fixed ruler is a translation: every point moves by the same amount in the same direction. A translated line keeps its direction, so the new line is parallel to the old one. The set square’s right angle is what guarantees perpendicularity.

3Circles and copying lengths 作圓與複製線段

Key point

Circles, arcs of given radii and line segments of given lengths can be constructed using a pair of compasses and a ruler.

A circle with centre O and radius 3 cm

  1. Open the compasses so that the gap between the point and the pencil is exactly 3 cm (measure it against the ruler).
  2. Press the needle point firmly into the paper at O.
  3. Hold the compasses by the top knob only: do not squeeze the legs.
  4. Turn the compasses all the way round in one smooth movement.
  5. Mark the radius on the diagram: a line from O to the circle, labelled 3 cm.

Copying a line segment

  1. Open the compasses so the gap equals the length of AB (needle on A, pencil on B).
  2. Without changing the opening, put the needle on the new point P.
  3. Draw an arc across the ray from P; call the crossing point Q.
  4. Then PQ = AB exactly: no measuring needed.

4Constructing a triangle 作三角形

Which tools you need depends on what you are given. There are five cases.

CaseWhat is givenToolsMethod
1 Three sides (SSS) ruler + compasses Draw one side; draw arcs of the other two lengths from its ends; join the crossing point.
2 Two sides and the included angle (SAS) ruler + protractor Draw one side, measure the angle at its end, mark the second side, join up.
3 Two angles and the included side (ASA) ruler + protractor Draw the side; measure one angle at each end; extend the two arms to meet.
4 Two angles and a non-included side (AAS) ruler + protractor Find the third angle first (∠ sum of △ = 180°), then follow Case 3.
5 A right angle, the hypotenuse and one side (RHS) ruler + protractor + compasses
(or set square)		 Draw the right angle, mark the given side, then strike the hypotenuse length from its end.
Which case am I in?

Count what you are told. 3 sides → Case 1. 2 sides + 1 angle between them → Case 2. 2 angles + the side between them → Case 3. If the side is not between the two angles → Case 4 (find the third angle first). If there is a right angle plus the longest side → Case 5.

Worked exampleConstruct △ABC with AB = 6 cm, BC = 4.5 cm and CA = 7.5 cm

This is Case 1 (three sides).

  1. Draw AB = 6 cm with the ruler.
  2. Open the compasses to 7.5 cm; put the needle on A and draw an arc above AB.
  3. Open the compasses to 4.5 cm; put the needle on B and draw an arc crossing the first one.
  4. Label the crossing point C. Join AC and BC.
  5. Check with a protractor: 7.5² = 56.25 and 6² + 4.5² = 56.25, so ∠A = 90°: this triangle is right-angled.

5Let’s check 課堂練習

Check yourself
  1. Which two tools do you need to construct a pair of parallel lines?
  2. Which tool do you use to copy a length exactly?
  3. You are told to construct △DEF with DE = 5 cm, DF = 2 cm and ∠FDE = 60°. Which case is this, and which tools do you need?
  4. You are told NP = 3.5 cm, ∠MNP = 80° and ∠NMP = 55° in △MNP. Which case is this? What must you calculate first?
  5. Why must you not erase your construction arcs?
  1. A ruler and a set square.
  2. A pair of compasses.
  3. Case 2 (two sides and the included angle): ruler and protractor.
  4. Case 4: the side NP is not between the two given angles (the angle at M does not touch NP). First find the third angle: ∠MPN = 180° − 80° − 55° = 45°, then construct with side NP and the angles at N (80°) and P (45°), that is Case 3.
  5. They are the evidence that your construction is accurate. Marks are given for the arcs being visible.

!Common mistakes 常見錯誤

✗ WrongErasing the arcs after constructing
✓ RightLeave every arc and construction line visible
The arcs prove the method. Without them the marker cannot tell a construction from a guess.
✗ WrongSliding the set square without holding the ruler
✓ RightHold the ruler firmly; slide only the set square
If the ruler moves, the “parallel” line is no longer parallel.
✗ WrongChanging the compass opening between the two arcs
✓ RightSet each length once and keep it
If the opening changes, the side lengths are wrong and the triangle will not match the data.
✗ WrongUsing the protractor’s outer scale on an acute angle and getting 140°
✓ RightCheck the reading against your estimate
Read from the 0 that sits on the arm you lined up.
✗ WrongDrawing a “perpendicular” freehand because it looks right
✓ RightUse the set square and mark the right-angle symbol
A construction must be exact. The square symbol also tells the marker what you intended.
One-line summary

Ruler + set square → parallel / perpendicular. Compasses + ruler → circles and copied lengths. Ruler + protractor (+ compasses) → triangles. Never rub out your arcs.
